BadSad, after a detour into an RPG world we decided to leave for later: "Let's go with all the classic games out there. :D" So BadLevel went on a tour of the shooters we grew up with. The rule for the trip: learn each tradition's habits, never copy its maps, names or art. Six stops.

Stop 1: the techbase (Doom)

BadSad showed some old automaps: "Can you see how tight they are? Not cramped: the layout." BadLevel's levels were islands on strings: only 20-38% of the area was floor, and a third of that was corridor. Squeezing the rooms closer together failed (the last rooms had nowhere left to go). Turning it around worked: start with one block of floor, slice it into plots, and fit the mission onto the plots with doors straight through shared walls. Floor went from 25% to 46%, corridors from 33% to 7%. Slime pools, bright and dim rooms, and the leftover plots stay solid, which gives the ragged outline.

Stops 2-4: a keep, an outpost, a grand hall (Quake 1, Quake 2, Unreal)

These three came out of dials BadLevel already had, plus one fix:

  • runekeep: a dark gothic keep with bits of base in it. Packed tight over three storeys, with lava and dim, uneven light.
  • outpost: a military-industrial base. Slime, catwalks, glass, big crates, lifts and ladders.
  • grand: everything big. Huge halls, open yards, tall windows.

The fix: on a packed plan, a staircase can't climb a whole storey inside a 3-cell wall, so those became stairwells. Runekeep went from needing up to 36 tries to build a level to at most 8.

Stop 5: the arena (Quake 3, Unreal Tournament)

Deathmatch is a different game. There's no start and no exit, just a circuit people run round and round, so BadLevel got a whole new mode:

  • No traps. Every room gets a second way out. The checker makes sure that from anywhere you can reach, you can get back to everywhere else, even after a drop.
  • Power-ups where the fights are. The quad goes in the most central room, up high when there's high ground. The armour and megahealth spread out, so there's more than one spot worth holding.
  • Fair spawns. Guns go down first, then every spawn is placed about the same walk from its nearest gun. The first try put one spawn 1 step from a gun and another 98 steps away (33 times unfair). Now it's at most 1.5 times.
  • Jump pads. Towers with no stairs and a pad that throws you on top. Before trusting the math, a player-sized box flew every arc through the level. 27 out of 27 landed.

Stop 6: the facility (Half-Life, Abiotic Factor)

What makes a research facility feel real is that every room is for something. So:

  • Departments. Each keycard door divides two sectors of the complex: admin at the entrance, the reactor at the bottom.
  • Rooms with jobs, each with blockout furniture: desks in offices, benches in labs, racks in server rooms, shelving in the warehouse, tables in the cafeteria, a core in the reactor. Every piece is named, so it can be swapped for real props later.
  • Vents. Some short loops become low ducts, 64 units high. Quake's player is 56 tall, so they just fit.

Bloopers: the first department plan gave one sector seven atriums. The "this is the big hall" marker had the same name as an older marker for rooms merged into one hall. And the first furniture rules were so careful about doorways that only 1 lab in 11 got its benches. Now it's about 85%. The first vent was 60 cells long too: less a short cut, more a hike on your knees.

Six stops, and every map compiles clean in Quake 1 and Quake 2 (120 out of 120 for each). Next: team maps with two mirrored halves, and big windows for the grand halls.
