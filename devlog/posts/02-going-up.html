Flat levels are fine until you remember Quake had verticality. So BadLevel learned storeys: stairwells that switch back, lifts, ladders, and one-way drops (the validator makes sure a drop never strands you). Then windows and glass, so you can see where you're going before you get there, and galleries: balconies in tall halls where the snipers live.

Long corridors got "beats" (a chamber, some alcoves, a sudden tall vault) so they stop feeling like spreadsheets. Rooms learned to merge into big halls. Courtyards opened to the sky. And mega-rooms arrived: a whole little level generated inside one giant room, with terraces and catwalks.

Finally, recipes. Instead of 40 knobs, you say what you want in design words, like bunker, fortress, industrial, and BadLevel turns it into settings. Plus a live website that rebuilds every level from the newest code on every push. (You're reading it.)
