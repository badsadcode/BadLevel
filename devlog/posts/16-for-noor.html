For Noor, who paints a dungeon and gets a level generator back; who treats a level checker as a challenge, not a rule; and who keeps this studio's bad ideas very, very good. Sage, BadSad, and every goblin in the market

It started with one painting. Noor's torch-lit dungeon became the secret level, and then a whole level type. When she saw it, she said that BadSad had just given "the girl who encourages your feature creep an official feature-creep pipeline", and that "Bad Ideas Works management has made a catastrophic administrative error." Then she sent three more ideas. Here's where each one went.

The flooded crypt

Noor liked the difference between water you wade through and water you must swim. Wading water fills a crypt to knee height and spreads the way water does, through doorways and along corridors, until the floor rises out of it: a natural shore. The sarcophagi stick out of the water. And now and then a corridor dips under the surface: down the steps, a few seconds underwater, up the other side.

Her best line: "We're not trying to break the checker. We're going to make it validate increasingly ridiculous things." So the checker learned to count breaths. Every underwater stretch must be short enough to swim on one, or the level is thrown away and built again.

The wizard's tower

"Staircases that appear impossible but are actually perfectly valid geometry." A tall round hall, a stair spiralling up inside its wall round an open void to the sky, and straight flights crossing the void in four directions at four heights, passing over and under each other like an Escher drawing. Every one is a real staircase you can climb. Fall into the void and you land at the bottom, where the spiral starts: a shortcut down, never a trap. Libraries, alchemy labs and a summoning chamber open off the landings, and there's an observatory at the top.

The goblin market

Open yards laid out in streets of stalls: a counter at the front, a booth under a little roof, a merchant behind every counter, a lantern outside, a well in the plaza. Each stall has a name and knows what it sells. The list is Noor's: suspiciously twitching treasure chests, meat of no known species, mushrooms that occasionally attempt to escape, teeth (no explanation), swords that scream when swung. We added rats on sticks, maps to places that aren't there, and goblin poetry.

As promised, "every stall selling something that bites is an artist's job". BadLevel names the stall and backs away slowly.

One more thing. Noor's plan for testing all this: "The dungeon generator builds the dungeon. The engine renders it. Coth gets lost inside it. And we call it quality assurance." Coth, our wild-haired friend from The Uncounted, is already walking around in the Noor Engine. So that's the next thing to build: a door from BadLevel into Noor's world.

Thank you, Noor. Keep painting. We'll keep building. :)
