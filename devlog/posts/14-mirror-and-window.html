Two promises were left over from the classic tour: team maps, and big windows for the grand halls. BadSad: "Let's do the mirrored team maps next 😃 oh yes !!!" Three exclamation marks. No pressure.

Part 1: the mirror

In a team game, nothing matters more than neither side getting an advantage. The classic capture-the-flag answer is simple: both bases are the same base, flipped. Building a symmetric level from scratch would mean teaching every step of the generator about symmetry. Instead, BadLevel builds one team's half as an ordinary deathmatch level, then holds a mirror up to it:

  • Lanes. Two or three straight lanes run from the half's open side to a seam line, where they meet their own reflections. The middle one widens into a midfield chamber with the only quad in the map.
  • Bases. The flag goes in the room that's the longest walk from the lanes. The team spawns go at the back of the base, around the flag. (The first try put some right by the lanes: you'd respawn in the enemy's path.)
  • The mirror. Every floor, ceiling, slope, diagonal wall, slime pool, item, jump pad, ladder and lift gets flipped across the seam. Facing directions turn round, and the flag and spawns change team. The tests check every cell: an exact mirror on every map.

Blooper: the compile check caught that gothic, bunker, egyptian and castle bases found no way out at all in 60 tries. Their halls have rounded corners and pilasters all along the walls, so no lane could leave through a flat wall. Now a lane may cut through the room's own pilasters, and every style works.

Part 2: the window

The Unreal-style halls were missing one old trick: a wall of glass with a whole world beyond it, so the castle seems to stand on a mountain. BadLevel now finds a stretch of a tall hall's outer wall with plenty of rock behind it, puts in a big glass window, and builds a view out there that nobody can ever walk to. The ground drops away under the window ledge, then smooth hills rise to the horizon, higher at the sides so the window frames a valley, with sky over everything.

A neat bit of maths kept the hills smooth: a sloped floor tile is only a valid Quake brush if its four corners lie on one flat plane. If the hill height is (how far out) plus (how far across), the corners always line up, so there are no lumpy tiles.

Bloopers: the first windows were sad little strips. The hall's ceiling curves down low near the walls, so the glass stopped halfway up a 400-unit hall. Now a window bay cuts up into the vault, and the glass runs nearly to the top. Pilasters also chopped the walls into pieces too short for any window, so 4 halls out of 6 got none (the same lesson as the CTF lanes, learned twice in one evening). Then the first view tried to build itself on top of a corridor, because one variable name was quietly reused. And beyond the hills the view ended in a grey wall, like the edge of a film set, so the rock around it is now drawn as sky.

Every map still compiles clean in Quake 1 and Quake 2 (128 out of 128 each). Next: Noor's dungeon. The secret level is about to stop being a secret.
