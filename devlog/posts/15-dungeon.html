Remember the secret level? Noor said "dungeon", BadSad remembered the creepy old dungeon crawlers, and Noor painted a torch-lit dungeon with a narrow bridge over spikes. We promised it was on the roadmap, "not now". Well. BadSad: "...then .. Dungeons! 😃" Now is now.

--recipe dungeon starts at a gatehouse and goes down through four parts: the keep, the prison, the catacombs, and the deep. Here's what's in it:

  • Bridges over spikes. A deep pit cuts across a hall, crossed by a bridge exactly two player-widths wide. The pit is deeper than any drop the level checker will walk, so falling in kills you but can never trap you. That's the whole trick of a good pit. Pillars in its way stay standing, rising out of the abyss.
  • Torches on the walls instead of lights hanging mid-air: warm, dim, about every six steps, never next to a doorway. In Quake 1 they're the game's real wall torches.
  • Grates in the ceiling, open to the sky, with a shaft of light falling through.
  • Traps in corridors: swinging blades, dart holes and spike floors, left as markers for each game to build its own way.
  • Rooms with jobs, like the facility: crypts full of sarcophagi, prison cells, libraries, armouries, shrines with an altar, a vault for the key, a great hall with a balcony (for the goblins), and a throne room on a dais.

Bloopers: the first chasm couldn't find a single room to fit in, across six whole dungeons. It wanted a strip of floor from wall to wall with no pillars and nowhere near a door, and castle halls are made of pillars and doors. Then it settled for sad little trenches along one wall that you could just walk past. Now it tries thirty spots and keeps the biggest pit, so every dungeon gets one or two real chasms. Also: the faint light in the middle of big rooms was hung at the room's floor height, which in a grand hall meant inside the stone plinth standing in the middle. One dungeon needed thirty tries before it gave up building its lights in rock.
