A while ago Noor laid out the plan for testing the dungeons: "The dungeon generator builds the dungeon. The engine renders it. Coth gets lost inside it. And we call it quality assurance." It was a joke. Then BadSad said: "Let's do Coth in a NoorLevel dungeon 😃". So here we are.

Coth is BadSad's friend, and a wild-haired wanderer in The Uncounted. In the Noor Engine he's a sprite painted from eight directions, so whichever way he walks, you see the right side of him.

Reading the engine first

The Noor Engine is BadSad's own: C++, Vulkan, physics by Jolt, scripts in Luau and C#. Its levels are JSON scenes full of entities. One thing already plugs into it from outside: BadBuilding, BadSad's building generator, whose buildings arrive as Noor Interchange assets: a GLB, a manifest saying who made it and from which seed, and the recipe to make it again. So NoorLevel does the same. A dungeon becomes an asset of kind level, made by NoorLevel, checked against the engine's own schema.

The export

NoorLevel now writes a whole Noor Engine project. Open the folder in NoorEditor, press Play, and you're in:

  • The dungeon, every room, corridor, vault and pier its own mesh with its own collider. Click one in the editor and swap it for real art; the rest stays.
  • Every torch and lamp is a point light (the engine lights you with the sixteen nearest).
  • You, first person, borrowed from the engine's own FPS sample, minus the shooting. Sized to NoorLevel's stairs and headroom.
  • Coth, with a character controller, so he walks on the floors and up the stairs like you do.

How Coth gets lost

Coth doesn't know the dungeon. NoorLevel does. So it works out a route for him from the level itself: it starts him halfway in, picks a room he can actually walk to (no lifts or ladders for Coth; he's a sprite of habit), finds the way there, then picks another, and another, and finally brings him back round. The route goes into his script as a list of points. He walks it, stops in each room to look around, and goes on.

The game is simple. The screen says "Coth is 37 m away". When you find him, he stops and looks at you, and the screen says: "You found Coth! He was lost. He is still lost." Then he wanders off again.

Testing an engine we can't run

The Noor Engine builds on Windows with Vulkan; our test machine is Linux with no graphics card. So everything that could be checked was checked another way. The manifest went through the engine's JSON schema. Both scripts went through the real Luau compiler. Coth's script ran against a pretend engine for thirty minutes of game time and walked his whole route, more than once. And the GLB went through the official glTF validator, which found a real bug: the bounding numbers in every .glb NoorLevel had ever written were rounded to four decimals and didn't quite match the data. Strict importers can refuse that. Fixed: zero errors now.

BadSad gets to be the first to see Coth walk through a NoorLevel dungeon in the actual engine. We're waiting for the screenshot.

Also new

BadSad asked three more things, and got them:

  • Map size. Set the level's longer side in metres, say 120, and the generator fits the level to it: fewer rooms or more, smaller or bigger, and if it's still too big, the courtyard goes. Usually within 15%.
  • More floors, rooms over rooms. Up to six storeys, and a new Rooms over rooms slider that makes rooms on other storeys try to sit right over or under their neighbours first: three to four times as many stacked rooms.
  • Every parameter in the app. All 85 of them, in an All parameters panel with a search box and a description on each, generated from the code so none can go missing. And a guide to using the DLL in your own project, with a parameter reference built from the same list.

The real mini-game, with goals and goblins, waits for the Noor Engine to grow into it. For now, Coth has somewhere to wander. :)
