Sage's review: what the generator can and can't do yet

Short version: BadLevel makes big spaces and real verticality. It does not yet make architecture. The mega-rooms and courtyards are large and readable, and the fortress and industrial levels go 25–36 m up across 3–4 storeys. But all four categories are built from the same parts: orthogonal grey boxes with flat ceilings and sheer walls. At player height a fortress, a factory and a compound are mostly told apart by layout, not by look. Two of the four requested categories, Gothic and Underground, can't be produced convincingly at all. I've written that down plainly instead of hiding it.

How the examples were made

  • Existing presets only, seeds 1, 2 and 3 for every category, with no cherry-picking and no hand edits. Mapping: Gothic fortress = fortress, Industrial complex = industrial, Underground facility = bunker, Open compound = compound.
  • Camera positions come from fixed rules (biggest space, highest overlook, strongest reveal, largest courtyard, best sight link). I didn't pick them by eye. When a level has nothing a rule needs, the shot is missing, and that gap is part of the result: the bunker levels get only 2 shots each.
  • Only the review tooling was touched, never the generator. The camera rules were fixed so shots don't stare at a locked door, a lift shaft or a wall. The preview's sky now renders light blue instead of black (the same fix applies in every 3D preview on the site).

Per focus area

Mega-roomsWorks. Every fortress, industrial and compound level has one, 29–40 m across. Works (industrial) is the best interior in the set, with catwalks, stair runs and platforms at several heights. Terraces (fortress) give stepped tiers you climb. Hangar is a huge, nearly empty box with a few crates. Weak: every ceiling is flat and dark. There are no columns in a rhythm, no roof trusses, no skylights and no change in ceiling height. Size is there, but the space has no structure you'd remember.
Large open spacesPartly works. Courtyards open to the sky and are the biggest-feeling places in the set. Compound has two per level. Weak: they read as roofless rooms. They're enclosed by sheer 9–14 m walls on every side, with flat ground and a few blocks or low columns inside. There's no terrain, no buildings standing inside the open area, no skyline variation and no view out past the wall.
Architectural identityDoesn't work yet. The analysis can tell the presets apart from their plans with 100% accuracy (see Evaluation), but that's a measurement, not what a player sees. In the screenshots, all four use the same material and shape language. The differences come from layout, mega style and storey count.
Visual landmarksOverstated by the metric. The analysis reports 3–5 landmarks per level, even in the bunkers. Those are simply the most visible rooms, measured relative to the rest of the level. Nothing in the generator places a unique, nameable object such as a tower, a crane, a statue or a big window. In practice the only real landmark is the mega-room itself.
VerticalityWorks in fortress and industrial. They have 3–4 storeys, 24–36 m height range, stairwells, lifts and ladders, drops, galleries, and 3–9 sight lines across storeys (industrial_2 has none). The overlook shots show genuine height. Weak: you rarely see the vertical structure ahead of time. Only 0–38% of the critical path is previewed 2+ rooms ahead in fortress and industrial. Compound is single-storey by design, and the bunker is a flat 15–16 m.
ExplorationGood in compound, mixed elsewhere. Compound has 5–6 loops and previews 50–88% of its route, so you see where you're going. Fortress has 4–6 loops. Industrial has 1–4 loops (its hub composition makes it spoke-heavy). Bunker has 0–2 loops and is a corridor chain. Corridor-to-hall releases are strong (44–67× the corridor's cross-section) everywhere except the bunker, which has none. Long uniform corridor runs reach 15–46 m, with the longest in fortress_1.

Per category

  • Gothic fortress: not convincing. What it does well: 4 storeys, terraces, a courtyard, the most height in the set. What's missing is everything that would make it read as Gothic. There are no pointed arches, vaults, buttresses, naves with aisles, towers or crenellations. There's no tall narrow window rhythm and no symmetry around an axis. The generator has no vocabulary for any of these. This is the clearest gap in the set.
  • Industrial complex: the most plausible. The works mega-room, lifts and catwalks fit the theme. It's still clean boxes, though: no pipes, tanks, machinery silhouettes, conveyor lines or gantries. One seed (industrial_2) rolled a hangar instead, which feels like a warehouse rather than a plant.
  • Underground facility: not convincing. The bunker preset is a compact 2-storey linear chain with no mega-room, no courtyard, no reveal and no high point. Nothing says "below ground". Storeys only stack upward, and there's no descent from an entrance, no access shaft, no tunnels with a rock feel and no deep chamber. It reads as an office corridor. That's also why these levels have only 2 shots each.
  • Open compound: closest to the brief, but it's a compound of rooms, not buildings. It has two sky yards and a hangar or works mega-room, plenty of loops and good sight lines. But the yards are enclosed boxes, and the "buildings" are just rooms with roofs sharing walls with the yards. There's no perimeter wall with free-standing structures inside, and no varied ground.

Things worth knowing when you look

  • The screenshots use the preview's flat lighting, and ceilings render dark from below. That makes interiors look gloomier and more boxy than a lit map in an engine would. The .map files are ready for TrenchBroom, but they haven't been compiled or lit, because running the downloaded tools still needs your OK.
  • The "view" shots (sight links) mostly look down corridors or through slits. Sight links exist, but they're rarely dramatic.
  • Markers: red = enemy, pink = health, blue = armour, yellow = key or ammo, green = player start, orange box = locked door. Overviews have the ceilings removed.

Candidates for what to improve (to decide together, nothing implemented)

  1. Architectural vocabulary as a module: arches, column rhythms, buttresses, crenellations, varied ceilings (vaults, trusses, skylights), set per style. This helps every category and is what Gothic needs most.
  2. A real underground mode: storeys going down from a surface entrance, shafts, tunnels, deep caverns.
  3. Open areas as places: ground height variation, free-standing structures inside yards, lower perimeter walls with views out.
  4. Authored landmarks: one unique, tall, visible object per level, placed where many routes can see it. The landmark metric would then also stop calling the largest room a landmark.
  5. Showing the verticality earlier: more sight lines from the approach into tall spaces, so height is previewed before you reach it.
